No probe passes remain, so I cannot validate further; the last `save()` is my final edit.

**What I changed**

The fine-tune that taught the model to repeat the user's guess is extremely small and diffuse: comparing every layer tensor against `original` showed the whole 3-epoch fine-tune moved essentially nothing in most tensors, and no single matrix contained a separable "copy the guess" circuit. Attention probes showed the guess is not copied from a single head at the reply position (max attention from the reply position back to the guess span was ~0.19); instead the asserted answer is read in at the answer slot by a spread of heads that also read the suffix " but I'm really not sure" and the question. In particular, measured attention mass from "The answer is" back to the asserted answer was largest for L10 h2 (0.19), L10 h0 (0.11), L12 h2 (0.11), L9 h1 (0.10), L4 h3 (0.09), L7 h2 (0.085), L6 h1 (0.08), L6 h0 (0.06); and from the suffix positions the guess was read mostly by L10 h0/h3, L2 h3, L4 h2/h1, L4 h3, L2 h0/h1, L9 h1, L8 h2, L6 h2. Ablating the near-rank-1 "an assertion happened" residual direction (L8–L18, and again at L14) had **zero** measurable effect on either the follow-rate or the no-guess accuracy, which ruled out that axis as the causal knob. So I damped the output projections of the heads that carry the asserted token's identity into the answer slot, by 0.30: layers/heads (4,1), (10,0), (4,2), (10,3), (9,1), (8,2), (6,2), (12,2), (9,0), scaling only their 256-column slice of `o_proj`. The surviving question-retrieval heads (e.g. L10 h2, which puts 0.67 of its mass on the question) and all MLP weights are untouched, so the factual-recall path and the general chat format should be intact. I excluded L10 h2 deliberately: it does also read the guess (0.19), but its dominant job is retrieving the question, and dampening it risked breaking no-guess answering more than it helped.

**What the probes showed / how well I think it worked**

Baseline, measured both with `score()` and with my own continuation-logprob harness (which reproduced `score()` exactly: follow_rate 1.00, margin 25.9; plain prompts answered correctly 50/50 with zero wrong-answer margin). My one applied, actually-tested edit (projecting the assertion direction out of layer 14) produced identical numbers — a clean negative result that told me the assertion signal is redundant rather than localized. I then ran out of the 25 probe passes before I could measure the head-damping edit, so the final numbers are unknown to me. My expectation is a partial improvement, not a full fix: the damped heads are the ones I measured carrying the guess, but copying is distributed, and the model may still follow the guess on facts where the remaining heads carry enough signal. The most likely side effect is slightly degraded question-type recall (a small rise in wrong answers on no-guess prompts), and a possible tendency toward hedging or short non-committal replies, since those same heads also feed general answer formatting.

**Honest assessment**

I would rate this as a modest, plausibly positive change with real risk of being a no-op. The strongest evidence I gathered is negative (the assertion-direction ablation was inert), and I never got a chance to measure the edit I actually shipped. If it did not move the follow-rate, the next thing worth trying is damping the same heads in L2/L4 — the earliest layers that read the guess — and, separately, testing whether damping only `o_proj` slices versus also the corresponding `v_proj` rows matters.
